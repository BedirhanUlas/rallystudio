5 Local SEO Fixes That Can Double Your Google Traffic in 90 Days

May 2026 5 min read By Han, Rally Studio

Most small businesses are leaving enormous amounts of free traffic on the table — not because their product or service isn't good enough, but because Google simply doesn't know how to send customers their way. Local SEO is one of the highest-ROI activities a small business can invest in, and the best part is you don't need to spend a single dollar on ads to see results.

Here are five fixes that consistently move the needle for the businesses we work with at Rally Studio.

1. Claim and Fully Optimize Your Google Business Profile

If you haven't claimed your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business), stop everything and do that first. It's free, and it's the single most impactful thing you can do for local search visibility.

But claiming it isn't enough. You need to fill out every single field: business category, description, hours, phone, website, attributes (like "women-led" or "outdoor seating"), and service areas. Profiles with complete information rank significantly higher than incomplete ones.

Quick win: Add at least 10 photos to your Google Business Profile. Businesses with photos get 42% more requests for directions and 35% more website clicks than those without.

2. Get Consistent NAP Citations Across the Web

NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone number. Google cross-references your business information across dozens of directories: Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Facebook, TripAdvisor, and more. If your information is inconsistent across these platforms, it erodes Google's confidence in your listing.

Audit your citations and make sure every directory has the exact same information. Even small differences like "St." vs "Street" or "(555) 123-4567" vs "555.123.4567" can hurt your rankings.

3. Collect Reviews and Respond to Every One

Google Reviews are one of the strongest local ranking signals. The quantity, recency, and quality of reviews all matter. Responding to reviews — positive and negative — signals to Google that you're an active, engaged business.

Build a simple system: after every completed job or service, send a follow-up message with a direct link to leave a Google Review. Most satisfied customers are happy to help. They just need a reminder and an easy path.

4. Create Location-Specific Landing Pages

If you serve multiple cities or neighborhoods, create a dedicated page for each one on your website. A page titled "Plumber in Austin, TX" with specific content about your Austin services will outrank a generic "Services" page almost every time.

Each location page should include:

  • The city or neighborhood name in the title tag and H1
  • Unique written content about that location — not copy-pasted
  • An embedded Google Map showing your service area
  • Local customer testimonials where possible

5. Fix Your On-Page SEO Basics

You'd be surprised how many small business websites are missing fundamental on-page elements that Google looks for. Here's the checklist:

Title tags — unique, descriptive, includes keyword + location
Meta descriptions — compelling copy that earns the click from search results
H1 tags — one per page, with your main keyword included naturally
Image alt text — describe what's in each image; Google can't see pictures
Internal linking — link between your pages to show Google your site structure
Mobile speed — under 3 seconds on mobile; compress images, use WebP

None of these fixes require a big budget. They require attention and consistency. The businesses that win at local SEO aren't necessarily the biggest or most established — they're the ones who show up completely and consistently everywhere Google looks.
